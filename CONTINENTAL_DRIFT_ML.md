# Continental Drift (Multilingual Learner version): teacher notes

A 13-question starter set for students who start with **no knowledge** of continental drift. Find it under **Starter sets → Earth Science → Continental Drift (Multilingual Learner version)**, or import `continental-drift-ml.set.json` (Today → Import).

It follows the same path as *Wegener's Case File*: words → look at the shapes → the four kinds of clue → Wegener's problem → a hypothesis → claim, evidence, reasoning. Every question is tagged `Continental drift` and `MS-ESS2-3`, plus a skill tag, so mastery and "who needs help" work as usual.

## The questions

| # | Type | What students do | Answer / what to look for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sort (feedback right away) | **Land or water?** Africa, South America, Asia, Antarctica vs four oceans | Continents → Land, oceans → Water |
| 2 | Sort (feedback right away) | **Word match**: coastline, fossil, mountain chain, evidence (a meaning and an example each) | Same words as the Word Bank on slide 1 |
| 3 | Draw on a map | Trace the two coastlines that face each other | East coast of South America and west coast of Africa |
| 4 | **Place It (model)** | Slide and **turn** South America so the coastlines fit Africa's | South America must be turned about 50° counter-clockwise (answer key 310°; any turn within 40° counts). Each piece counts as right within 120 units of its spot |
| 5 | Sort | **What kind of clue?** Coastline / Fossil / Mountain chain / Rocks and glaciers | The four rows of the Evidence Board |
| 6 | Draw on a map | Mesosaurus: X on each continent where fossils were found | South of Africa and south-east South America (Brazil, Uruguay). Look for the reasoning in the frame: it could not swim a salty ocean |
| 7 | Draw on the joined map | One long mountain chain across the south of both continents | Cape Fold Belt (South Africa) matches Sierra de la Ventana (Argentina) |
| 8 | **Place It (model)** | Put an arrow on each continent and turn it to show how it moved | Arrows pointing away from each other. Africa about east / north-east, South America about west / south-west (within 55°) |
| 9 | Sort | **Wegener's big idea**: what he had vs what he did not have | Slide 5, question 1 |
| 10 | Draw | **Hypothesis**: what could push or pull a continent? | No wrong answer. Ideas worth following up: heat, currents inside Earth, the ocean floor |
| 11 | Sort | **Build a good answer**: Claim / Evidence / Reasoning | Sets up question 12 |
| 12 | Draw (lined) | Claim, evidence, reasoning with the sentence frames | Level 1 of slide 6 |
| 13 | Draw on a map | Challenge: where will the continents be in 50 million years? | The Atlantic gets wider (about as fast as fingernails grow) |

Not every class needs all 13. A shorter route: 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 12.

## Language supports built in

- Short sentences, everyday words, one idea per sentence, no idioms. New science words are **bold** the first time they appear, in the same order as the Word Bank.
- Every Draw question ends with a **Sentence frame**.
- Picture first: six of the thirteen questions have a map or a movable piece (3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 13), and the vocabulary sorts (1, 2) are text-light with feedback right away.
- Sort cards are short (under 60 characters). Each card has an idea tag, so results by idea show *which clue type* or *which word* to reteach.
- Many words are Spanish cognates: continent / continente, ocean / océano, fossil / fósil, evidence / evidencia, hypothesis / hipótesis, mountain / montaña. If you tell me which home languages your class has, I can add a version with a word list or a home-language glossary.

## Accuracy notes

- The mountain match in question 7 is the Cape Fold Belt and Sierra de la Ventana. Question 5 uses the Appalachians and Scotland, as your Evidence Board does.
- Lystrosaurus is left out of the Africa / South America questions on purpose: it was not found in South America.
- The Gondwana glacier clue says "India" in questions 5 and 11. South America, Africa and Australia also have glacier marks.
- The motion in question 8 is a simplification: the Atlantic opened by turning, not straight sliding.
